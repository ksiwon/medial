The tool · What I am building
02

MEDial: an AI Care Orchestrator
that started from interviews in Eunjeom Village.

In the interviews, I kept seeing the same gap. When someone doesn't answer a check-in call, or needs to get to hospital, who checks on them, and who helps next? That question became MEDial.

MEDial is an AI Care Orchestrator. When someone doesn't answer, or needs a lift or care, it finds a neighbour or a public service to check and help, using only what people agreed to share. Today, people do this with phone calls and home visits.

A rural service needs to be tested and fixed many times, but you can't keep going back to the village. So I built a Village Simulator from the interviews and field notes of the 12 participants. A service designer runs MEDial inside it and reads what each resident went through.

Village Simulator · Replaying a day
03

Run the Village Simulator,
and a day in the village plays out.

You can see who goes where, and who MEDial asked to check in or give a lift. You can pause at any time.

The bar at the bottom runs from 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. Each request that is under way gets its own colour, so you can follow it.

Nothing on screen is made up. Schedules, relationships and places come from the interviews. What we don't know stays blank, because guessing would give us a village that looks right but is wrong.

This week, I wrote down for each of the 12 people what they know, what their day looks like, and who they are close to.

The tool · Two: Reading each person's experience
04

At ten at night,
each agent looks back
on its own day.

Each agent only rates what happened to it. When it doesn't know something, it says “I don't know”, and that answer is kept as it is.

There are no scores and no rankings. We read one person at a time, side by side.

On paper a request can be solved, while one neighbour spent over an hour on it and someone was sent without being asked. One number won't show that, so the tool shows each person separately.

The tool · Three: Run it again, change one setting
05

The designer reads the agents' evaluations
and picks one change.

The designer picks one problem from the residents' accounts and changes the setting behind it. Here: if a second call gets no answer, go to the health centre instead of a neighbour.

An example reason

“If they still don't pick up, pass it to the health centre. Neighbours shouldn't have to keep checking.”

Then the same day runs again with only that setting changed. Everything else is the same, so any difference comes from this setting. We check that the setting was actually used, then whether neighbours were asked less and whether more people waited on the health centre. If the situation never came up, we record that.

What exists and what comes next
06

To make a 10-day experience,
the Village Simulator needs three more things.

Right now, routines follow the interviews, set rules decide who helps and how people rate the day, and it runs one day at a time.

1. Resident behaviour

From rules to agents

Whether someone can go is still checked by place, schedule and travel time. Within that, persona agents decide how to answer and how to rate their day.

2. Making changes

Three changes MEDial can really make

From that day's events and ratings, it offers three changes MEDial can actually make, each with a reason.

3. Carrying into the next day

Days that follow on

Unfinished requests, time spent and settings carry over. When different choices lead to the same state, they merge, so every branch can be worked out in advance as a state graph.

The exhibition
07

At the exhibition, visitors become
service designers reviewing MEDial
over ten days in the village.

The tool is made for service designers. At the exhibition, that job is cut down to ten days and handed to visitors. Each day, a visitor picks one change and sees how the next day turns out.

Earlier, we ran the same day again with one setting changed. The exhibition works differently: choices build up, so each day is the result of everything chosen so far. At the end, visitors see their choices and what happened on one page.

The venue
09

One wide projector and one tablet
show what the next day brings.

A wide projector puts the Village Simulator on the wall. Requests, who MEDial is asking, and how residents are doing are all on one screen. After each choice, the next day plays right there.

The tablet is only for choosing. The change and the reason are both picked from a list, so each day takes under 15 seconds. That's about 10 minutes a person, up to six an hour. People waiting can watch on the wall.
